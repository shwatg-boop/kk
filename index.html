Chapter 2 • Post-Harvest Microbiology

Fermentation & Cherry Processing Methods

Inside a ripe red coffee cherry, two green seeds sit wrapped in parchment, sticky sugar-rich mucilage, and outer fruit skin. How farmers remove—or ferment—those fruit layers radically alters sweetness, body, clarity, and winey/tropical funk.

Chapter 3 • Thermodynamics & Maillard Reactions

Roasting: From Ultra-Light Cinnamon to Dark French

Green coffee beans are dense and grassy. Inside a rotating drum roaster (188°C–240°C), amino acids and sugars react (Maillard reaction), sucrose caramelizes, steam pops the bean open at First Crack (💥 ~198°C), and cellulose walls fracture at Second Crack (💥💥 ~224°C).

🌾 Light (Origin & Acidity Preserved) 🍯 Medium (Peak Caramel & Maillard Balance) 🔥 Dark (Roast Bitterness & Smoky Body)
Chapter 4 • Surface Area & Extraction Physics

Grinding & Extraction: Coarser for Filter, Finer for Espresso

Hot water dissolves coffee compounds in a strict sequence: 1. Sparkling Acids & Fruit (fast) → 2. Sweet Sugars (medium) → 3. Bitter Tannins & Plant Fibers (slow). High-pressure Espresso (~28 sec) needs a Fine grind (250–380 µm) to expose massive surface area fast, while gravity Pour-Over / Immersion Filters (2.5–4 min) require a Medium-to-Coarse grind (600–1,100 µm) so water doesn't over-extract harsh bitterness!
